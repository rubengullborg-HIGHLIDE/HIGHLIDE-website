## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** The interface does not immediately look AI-generated because real product data and detailed Aarhus stock information give it substance. The brand layer does contain familiar generated-fashion tells: a cream surface, oversized uppercase hero, burgundy pills, generous rounding, and floating clothing cutouts. The result is pleasant but not yet distinctly HIGHLIDE or distinctly Aarhus.

**Deterministic scan:** One warning was found: `flat-type-hierarchy` in `src/layouts/SiteLayout.astro:170`, associated through the shared layout with ten pages. The detector reports 12px, 14px, and 16px sizes at a 1.3:1 ratio while recommending at least 1.25, so this is likely a false positive rather than a confirmed defect. Manual judgment should focus on hierarchy across the whole page, not that isolated navigation cluster.

**Visual overlays:** No reliable user-visible overlay is available. The browser surface only exposed read-only evaluation, so mutable injection was not attempted, the live detector server was not started, and no overlay is being claimed.

## Overall Impression

The catalogue proves the concept; the surrounding experience does not yet explain, frame, or complete it. The biggest opportunity is to turn HIGHLIDE from “a product grid with future pages” into one coherent journey: understand the local promise, find an item, trust its availability, and continue to the shop.

## What’s Working

- The product overview creates immediate credibility through real volume, prices, sale states, store names, colours, and Aarhus availability.
- Product detail’s size-by-store inventory is unusually useful for a local-fashion prototype and makes the concept tangible.
- The shared layout, restrained palette, clear Danish labels, and reusable placeholder shell give the project a consistent baseline that can be evolved without a large architectural rewrite.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The core journey stops at product detail

**Why it matters:** HIGHLIDE promises to connect discovery to a local shop. After a user verifies that an item is in stock, there is no dominant next action to visit the shop, open directions, or continue to the source.

**Fix:** Place one primary action directly beside price and availability: “Se hos [butik]” when a reliable source URL exists, otherwise “Find butik” with address/directions. Keep the inventory timestamp or uncertainty beside this action.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] Navigation advertises unavailable features

**Why it matters:** Butikker, Profil, Favoritter, Log ind, and Hjælp & kontakt are presented as real destinations, yet most terminate in “kommer snart.” This makes the drawer feel like a prototype index rather than a confident product navigation.

**Fix:** Keep only functional destinations in primary navigation. Defer Profile, Favorites, and Login until the auth value proposition is real. Turn Butikker and Help/Contact into useful pages before promoting them.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

### [P1] Desktop navigation hides the product structure

**Why it matters:** A hamburger on a wide screen hides the two destinations users need most and wastes available header space. It also removes active-location feedback.

**Fix:** Keep the drawer below the desktop breakpoint. On desktop, show Forside, Produkter, and Butikker as visible links with a restrained active state. Keep informational/legal links in the footer. Add account actions only when they work.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P1] The custom zoom model competes with shopping decisions

**Why it matters:** “Zoom niveau 1–4” asks shoppers to learn a custom density system, and different levels hide different metadata. That increases recall and can weaken comparison, particularly on mobile.

**Fix:** Use a stable responsive grid or a familiar two-state “Kompakt / Detaljeret” control. Keep image, product name, price, shop, and availability consistently visible. Give sort and active filters a compact persistent row.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] Mobile-first behavior has not been visually verified

**Why it matters:** The product’s primary audience is mobile, but the browser viewport request did not produce a real narrow layout. Source rules include three-column small-screen arrangements and metadata hiding, which need genuine 320–430px testing.

**Fix:** Validate 320, 375, 390, and 430px widths. Default to two scannable cards; offer one-card detail mode only when intentional. Keep 44px touch targets and make filters reachable in the thumb zone.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] The homepage describes a category, not a distinct service

**Why it matters:** “Find din stil” could belong to almost any fashion search product. Users do not immediately understand that HIGHLIDE searches across smaller local shops and helps them locate stock nearby.

**Fix:** Introduce one concrete promise and a short real sequence directly below search: search across local assortments, verify Aarhus availability, then visit the shop. Show real product/shop evidence instead of three generic icon cards.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The brand is not yet specifically Aarhus-local

**Why it matters:** Beige, burgundy pills, and floating product cutouts are pleasant but interchangeable. Local credibility should be the differentiator.

**Fix:** Use authentic storefronts, streets, neighbourhood names, shop stories, product counts, and source attribution. Let actual local material carry the identity.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable delight`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan, first-time visitor:** The first screen says fashion search but does not clearly explain cross-store local inventory. Several visible navigation items lead to temporary pages, “zoom niveau” requires learning, and Help/Contact cannot provide help.

**Casey, distracted mobile user:** Narrow-width behavior is unverified; filters expose a long taxonomy; top-positioned controls and missing return-state preservation make interruption costly; product detail lacks a thumb-friendly final action.

**Riley, stress tester:** Stock is presented alongside a disclaimer but without prominent freshness or a direct recovery route. Error states lack retry controls, generic social links weaken trust, and polished placeholder destinations feel broken rather than intentionally deferred.

## Cognitive Load and Emotional Journey

The site has moderate cognitive load, with four checklist failures: single focus, minimal choices, one thing at a time, and progressive disclosure. The catalogue combines query, facets, sort, sale state, price, and a four-level zoom model. The emotional high point is seeing real local stock; the low point arrives immediately afterward when there is no actionable shop hand-off. The footer and placeholder routes then reinforce “prototype” precisely when the experience should close with trust.

## Direct Response to the Proposed Direction

### Desktop header

Agree. Use visible desktop links for Forside, Produkter, and Butikker, while retaining the burger drawer on mobile. Informational pages belong in the footer, and auth-related links should remain absent from prominent navigation until they create real value.

### Homepage explainer

Agree, with one caution: avoid three identical icon cards. This is a real sequence, so a three-step horizontal/stacked component is appropriate: “Søg lokalt,” “Se lager i Aarhus,” and “Find butikken.” Pair the steps with one real product-to-shop example, store imagery, or live catalogue facts so it explains both the mechanism and the vision.

### Butikker

Agree. Store cards are a legitimate card use because each shop is a distinct entity. Each card should expose useful information without an extra click: shop name, neighbourhood/address, short point of view, selected brands or categories, and product count. Make the primary action “Se produkter” and link to the product overview with that store filter encoded in the URL. A later store-detail page can add hours and a richer story; a click-only reveal is unnecessary for v1.

### Products

Preserve the catalogue’s overall direction. It is the strongest proof of value. Focus changes on a stable card hierarchy, familiar density controls, filter state, mobile validation, and the final shop hand-off rather than a visual redesign.

### Profile and favourites

Agree with deferring them. Keep the routes available for development and `noindex`, but remove them from the product’s primary navigation until auth, persistence, and the reason to create an account are ready.

### About, help, contact, and privacy

Agree on a shared system, not necessarily a single combined page. Create an “Information” footer group with Om HIGHLIDE, Hjælp & kontakt, and Privatlivspolitik. Use one reusable information-page layout for title, introduction, content sections, contact/action block, and optional last-updated metadata. Keep Privacy as a dedicated page so it can be linked directly from forms, analytics consent, and future auth screens.

## Minor Observations

- The footer social links currently point to generic social-network homepages rather than HIGHLIDE profiles.
- The homepage exposes filters before users have seen the catalogue; search should be primary there.
- The drawer has no active-route indicator.
- Store and brand sometimes duplicate on product cards, creating noise.
- “Produkt overblik” should be “Produktoversigt”; “Mindre standard browsing” sounds translated rather than natural Danish.
- Wide soft shadows and generous rounding slightly conflict with the sharper fashion direction.

## Questions to Consider

- Is HIGHLIDE’s primary promise “discover local fashion” or “verify local stock”? The homepage hierarchy should choose one as the lead and make the other supporting proof.
- Should store imagery feel documentary and neighbourhood-specific, or more polished and campaign-like?
- Is zoom solving a recurring shopper need, or compensating for cards whose information hierarchy changes too much?
